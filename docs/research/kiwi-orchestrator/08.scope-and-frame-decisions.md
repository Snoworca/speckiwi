# 2.6.0-phase1-kiwi-orchestrator scope, and the tie-rung/charter conflict — committee decisions

Date: 2026-07-31
Decided by: a five-member decision committee, each member researching in isolation.
Source: workflow `orchestrator-frame-committee`, run `wf_15d4d3d2-271`.
Both questions **unanimous, 5-0**.

This document is binding and supersedes the scope implied by `05.orchestrator-design.md` revision 3.

## 1. The measurement that decided it

The committee was told to **count** where the critical findings sit rather than assume, because that
was the decisive evidence. All five counted independently, from the primary sources, and agreed:

| | Measured |
|---|---|
| CRITICAL rows across the three revision rounds | 36–39 |
| In the lane / worktree / merge / epilogue execution machinery | **33–37 — about 92–95%** |
| Rounds 2 and 3 considered alone | **100% in that region** |
| Defects created by a revision's own fixes | **11 of 11 in lane execution, worktree lifecycle or merge** |
| Anything outside that region that regressed across three rounds | **none** |

Corroborating structure: §5, the parallelism model, is **1,585 of 6,445 lines (25%)**, with Profile A
alone at 296; the word "lane" occurs 1,202 times and "worktree" 170; roughly **31–34 of the 52–55**
orchestrator-owned gates are lane, worktree, merge or epilogue specific; and three of the five
escalated user rulings are parallelism-driven.

The single CRITICAL that is **not** parallel-caused is the same one in every round: critic 2's
`test_id: null` hole, where a handoff declaring every acceptance criterion untested lets a lane pass
every audit and promote a requirement with no executable evidence. It survives a serial cut and must
be fixed regardless.

One member reproduced a round-3 finding directly rather than reasoning about it: `git checkout -B`
from a second worktree onto a branch another worktree holds fails with
`fatal: … already used by worktree at …`.

A finer cut, which is why the decision goes further than "drop Profile A": of the 33, only about
**11–12 are worktree- or concurrency-specific**. The other **~22 are general to the lane model** and
survive even under a sequential in-tree profile. Cutting only worktree isolation would therefore have
left two thirds of the defect population in place. The **lane executor as a whole** is what goes.

## 2. Q1 — the tie rung: **`drop-rung-halt-on-no-majority`** (5-0)

The 3 → 5 escalation the earlier committee reinstated on a "no majority exists" trigger is dropped.
A gate where the committee produces no majority escalates to `critical` and **halts for the user**.

The charter's line — *"the unanimity requirement and the 3 → 5 escalation ladder are removed"* — is
honoured literally. No charter amendment is proposed.

### `07`'s Q2 is re-decided, not merely carried forward

`07` §3b made its unanimous `leave-all-three` answer conditional on the tie rung existing. The
condition fails on its literal wording. The committee re-decided Q2 anyway and it **stays
`leave-all-three`**, for a reason that only became visible once the rung was gone:

§3b's substance names *one-member-alone settlement* as the unacceptable outcome. Halting for the user
removes that outcome outright — more completely than the rung did. Meanwhile promoting the three
gates to `critical_gates[]` would make them halt on **every** invocation, including a unanimous 3-0 —
strictly more halting than today, in exactly the mode `07` §4.1 already established the user asked to
lighten. That is a larger deviation from the charter than the tie rung was.

**Two conditions travel with this decision and must be carried verbatim into the SRS.** They, not
`critical_gates[]` membership, are the real boundary on the affected population — `07:88-92` says so
explicitly:

- `07` §3a — under a split vote, "the confidence" is the **minimum within the winning bloc**, after
  the §4.1.1 adjustments.
- `07` §4.1 — fold §4.1.2's confidence-spread rung into §4.1.1's threshold mechanism. No re-vote on
  spread alone.

Consequences to carry forward: `05`'s A7 becomes DECLINED rather than PENDING; `AC-E20-4` is the
shipped tie criterion with no `AC-A7-1`; and `decideAutoGate` still takes a `tieRung: boolean`
parameter so that a later reversal stays additive rather than structural.

## 3. Q2 — 2.6.0-phase1-kiwi-orchestrator scope: **`cut-parallelism-to-next-target`** (5-0)

### What ships in 2.6.0-phase1-kiwi-orchestrator

- **Intake**: QnA for thin intent; ingestion of a research document, design document or GitHub issue;
  parallel investigation of the existing code and architecture; design deepening with real user
  questions.
- **The design document** at body scope, with marked `[D-nnn]` items, the normative-prose gate, and a
  design freeze before any implementation verb.
- **Wave decomposition** and a per-wave **English wave design document** with its own verification
  loop.
- **Per-stage English handoff documents for coding agents** — the full schema, the five-layer
  validator, the verification loop, the English-only gate, and the untested-acceptance-criterion cap
  with a required reason and owner. That cap is what closes the one non-parallel CRITICAL.
- **The per-task parallelisability analysis, in full**, as a frozen, published, reviewed artifact per
  wave: the pure `computeLanePlan` with its declared inputs, the closed conflict-reason enum, stage
  layering, coupling analysis, `files[]` grounding, and the partition postmortem fed forward.
- **The wave-boundary issue protocol**: closed classification, a plan document, a resolution proof per
  issue, and the rule that the next wave cannot start until each issue is terminal.
- **Compaction survival**: the wave journal with an executable parser and validator, the resume card
  with an invariant digest, a run contract document, write-ahead intent and write-behind result
  discipline, validate-on-every-append, and refuse-on-resume.
- **Post-merge verification** against the wave design, the frozen design items, the constraints and
  the original intent; with `validate` then `sync-index` mandatory after every merge per charter C4.
- **Requirement promotion** at the host root with its two-step transition and evidence calls.
- **The `--auto` contract change** per §2 above, including all eight `--auto` contract edits and the
  two-requirement SRS shape.
- **The harvest** from the deferred branch, and the missing `docs/next/` entry.
- **The executable substrate** in `src/core/orchestrator/**` with coverage thresholds, the
  `speckiwi orchestrate` CLI namespace, and the `orchestrate_*` MCP family.

Note what this means for the user's stated priority. They said dividing the tasks correctly at the
start is the most important step. **That analysis ships in full** — frozen, published and reviewed.
What defers is executing its output concurrently.

### What defers to 2.6.0-phase2-parallel-lanes

- **Concurrent execution of any kind.** 2.6.0-phase1-kiwi-orchestrator delivers **no wall-clock speedup**. Implementation runs
  serially over the wave plan on the integration branch, as `kiwi-wave-master` does today.
- **Worktree isolation entirely** — the profile, the isolation probe, acquire/release/reap, the
  heartbeat, lane timeouts, the join loop, liveness, the remediation branch family, per-worktree
  `npm ci` with its `NODE_ENV` pin and concurrency controls, the Windows path budget, and the
  consumer `.gitignore` remediation.
- **The lane execution apparatus** — per-lane dispatch, the lane commit obligation, the claim audit's
  eleven rows, the pre-merge per-lane loop, per-lane merge with the compare-and-swap publish,
  per-path restore on integrate, the deferred SRS-mutation queue and its replay, and the serial
  epilogue as a distinct executor.
- With them, **twenty** `critical_gates[]` entries (`05` §13's rows marked **2**), and **user ruling X-01 in its entirety** —
  which retires one of the three charter-level blockers the triage said had to be settled first.

### Why the two decisions reinforce each other

Every member noted the same coupling, in the same direction. The tie rung's only strong practical
argument is that halting mid-run strands live background lanes. Under a serial scope there are no
live lanes at a gate: a halt costs one prompt in a session the user is already attending, and the
working tree is intact. **The strongest argument for keeping the rung dies with the scope decision.**

One member added a concrete cost this repository has already paid: background teammate names are
runtime-session-scoped, so after a session resume every previously spawned agent is unreachable
**while still holding its worktree** — and six stale worktrees sit on this checkout right now as
evidence. Under full parallelism, a halt can strand up to eight of those.

## 4. This is a reduction against what the user asked for

The user asked, in their own words, for tasks in a wave to be checked for parallelisability, for the
parallelisable ones to be split into separate worktrees, and for those to be verified, improved and
merged. **2.6.0-phase1-kiwi-orchestrator will not do the last three.** It will do the analysis, publish it, and execute
serially.

The committee's case is that 92–95% of the critical defects live in the machinery being deferred, that
rounds 2 and 3 produced defects *exclusively* there, and that three adversarial rounds failed to
converge on it while nothing outside it regressed at all. That is an argument from measurement, not
from caution.

It is also reversible. The analysis artifact is the input the deferred executor will consume, the
isolation interface is already designed, and `decideAutoGate` keeps its `tieRung` parameter so a
reversal on §2 stays additive. Nothing here forecloses 2.6.0-phase2-parallel-lanes.
