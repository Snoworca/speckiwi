## 0. The decision in three sentences

The orchestrator classifies a piece of work onto one of three **shells** — `R-STEP` (delegate the whole
run to `/kiwi-tdd`), `R-PLAN` (delegate to `/kiwi-pm` over an already-existing contract-valid plan, then
`/kiwi-review-fix-loop --close-reqs`), or `R-ORCH` (the orchestrator runs its own intake → design →
decompose → per-wave flow over the shared wave engine) — by evaluating a **frozen route probe** through a
pure function that only ever *removes* rungs, never selects one, and then taking the first survivor in the
fixed order `R-PLAN → R-STEP → R-ORCH`.

**The number of waves is not a routing input.** It is a Phase 2 measurement made from the frozen design,
because the only component in the suite that could produce it has an output range of 3–8 sub-goals
(`skills/claude/kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:161`) and therefore structurally cannot answer "one".

**`R-ORCH` is total** — no predicate removes it — so the ladder always terminates, and the two cheap rungs
are reached only by clearing every disqualifier that would make them halt.

## 1. What the three routes actually are, and why the wave count is not the question

The charter names three routes and immediately warns they are not siblings (`00.charter.md:81-83`). The
warning is correct and it goes further than the charter states: two of the three are **delegations that end
the orchestrator's run**, and the third is **the orchestrator's own run**. Framed that way, the routing
question stops being "how many waves" and becomes "which shell owns this run".

| Rung | Shell | The orchestrator's role | First irreversible act |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R-STEP` | `/kiwi-tdd` | classifies, dispatches, ends | `kiwi-tdd` commits its red tests (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:84`) |
| `R-PLAN` | `/kiwi-pm` → `/kiwi-review-fix-loop --close-reqs` | classifies, dispatches, then runs a plan-coverage close-out | the first child `kiwi-coder` `update_status(…, "in_progress")` — an SRS `status` mutation, and therefore landed state under §7.5 (`kiwi-pm/SKILL.md:31`, `kiwi-coder/SKILL.md:538`). **No commit precedes it**: `kiwi-pm` never auto-commits (`kiwi-pm/SKILL.md:863`) and §5.2 passes no `--commit-lane-work` (§5.2 code block, `09:555`). `kiwi-pm` spawns Tasks sequentially in declaration order (`kiwi-pm/SKILL.md:8`, loop at `:327-328`) |
| `R-ORCH` | the orchestrator itself, over the extracted `_shared` wave engine | owns intake, design, decomposition, per-wave delegation, post-merge verification | `register-wave-srs` at Phase 3.b — the run's **first SRS mutation** (`05.orchestrator-design.md §1.4`) |

`R-ORCH` is the honest form of "route to wave-master". Per D1 (`00.charter.md:108`) the overlapping
machinery moves into `_shared` modules and `kiwi-wave-master` becomes a thin caller over it, so "choose the
wave shell" means "this run is the orchestrator's own, driven over that engine" — not "spawn the sibling
skill". §5 settles that ruling in full.

Inside `R-ORCH`, the wave count `N` is decided at Phase 2.a from the frozen design
(`05.orchestrator-design.md §2.1 2.a`), and `05`'s refusal floor already evaluates the "is this worth the wave
apparatus" question at the **end of Phase 2** — deliberately, "so it can still save the expensive part"
(`05.orchestrator-design.md §1.4`). That is the right place for it, because that is the first moment the
measurement exists. Putting a wave-count guess in the intake classifier would move a measurable decision to
a point where only a proxy is available, and every proxy considered was broken:

- The splitter's non-structured branch returns **3–8** coherent sub-goals (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:161`).
  It cannot return one. Asking it "how many waves" is a leading question whose range excludes the cheap answer.
- The headers-first branch adopts header/section boundaries as wave boundaries and names **plain top-level
  section titles** among its examples (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:160`). A five-heading research memo —
  Background / Problem / Proposal / Risks / References — would route to a five-wave run, registering five
  SRS targets (`:211`, `:225`) for one small feature.

Both heuristics are sound *inside* `kiwi-wave-master`, where the user has already declared the work large by
invoking it (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:3`'s trigger vocabulary). Transplanted into an unconditional intake
classifier they lose that precondition and inherit none of its calibration.

## 3. The classifier, in full

### 3.1 Shape

`computeRoute(probe, opts) → RouteDecision` is a **pure, total** function in
`src/core/orchestrator/route.ts`. There is no `src/core/orchestrator/` directory today — verified.

It is **disqualifier-first**: every predicate *removes* rungs and none selects one. The consequence is the
property the whole design is built to buy — **a wrong route can always be traced to one named predicate and
one recorded value.**

The rung is computed by `computeRoute` and never derived by a committee. The gate's ballot is
**closed over the classifier's own outputs** — the selected rung and `decision.alternative`, both of
which survived every disqualifier, or `abort` when `alternative` is `null` (§8.3) — so a committee or
a user may choose between two computed survivors, but neither can introduce a rung the classifier did
not produce, and every option on the ballot is traceable to the recorded probe. `auto-option.md:190`
would default an *undeclared* gate to `business-decision`, so the gate is declared explicitly rather
than left to that default (§8.1).

**S3 is the design's central measurement, and it replaces a broken one.** An earlier proposal used
`search_requirements` over the intent's `key_entities[]`. That call is a literal, case-insensitive `indexOf`
over ten text fields including `traceLinks` and `verificationEvidence`
(`src/core/query/discovery.ts:138-151`, `:161-173`), and the probe took the **union** over a list of terms —
so a common token saturates (nearly every requirement matches) and a novel vocabulary empties (no
requirement matches). `traceReference` is a different mechanism: `normalizedReferencesInclude` compares the
query against the requirement's normalized trace-anchor list for an **exact** match
(`src/core/query/filter.ts:12-17`, `:35`). It answers a precise question — *"which body requirements already
own this file?"* — and its output is a **list of requirement ids written into the probe**, so a wrong route
names the requirement that should have blocked it.

**S3's known blind spot is measured, not assumed.** `traceReferences` is populated from **every** Trace Links
row regardless of `type` (`src/core/query/records.ts:124` does not filter on `TraceLink.type`), so it cannot
by itself tell a code anchor from a `Requirement` or `Task` row. That is why S3c is type-qualified — it counts
only `traceLinks[]` rows whose `type == "Code"`, which is what `kiwi-coder` writes — and that qualification is
the reason S3c guards the blind spot rather than restating it. In a repository whose requirements were never
implemented through `kiwi-coder`, S3 is empty everywhere and would clear `R-STEP` vacuously. `S3c` is the guard: when `anchor_coverage < 0.2`, S3 is marked low-confidence, D1 may not clear
`R-STEP` on the measurement alone (the declared-judgment half must also be clean), and the gate's
`recommended` marker is withheld so the decision goes to a committee or a human.

**Not inputs, deliberately.** `existing_srs_context.json.similarity_score` (`kiwi-srs/SKILL.md:306`) is an
analyst-authored float with no calibration anywhere in the suite; it is recorded in the probe and drives no
threshold. `S9.summary` counts requirements **already in the target**, not incoming work
(`src/core/types.ts:296-299`) — a legitimate denominator, a misleading proxy. `S5.files` is recorded for the
gate's evidence table (§8.3) and the user-facing prose (§9.1) and drives no threshold either, because no
calibrated magnitude threshold exists anywhere in the suite and the magnitude decision is owned by the refusal
floor and `09` §7.4's downgrade gate at the end of Phase 2, where the measurement exists. The wave splitter is
not called (§1).

**Work-mode is not a disqualifier.** See §4. This is the single largest departure from every earlier
proposal and it is required by `00.charter.md:71`.

**`--auto` is not a disqualifier either.** See §4.4.

### 3.4 Selection

```
order: R-PLAN → R-STEP → R-ORCH        first surviving rung wins
```

- **`R-PLAN` first** because its surviving preconditions are now genuinely the strongest: a contract-valid
  plan, on the active target, with open Tasks, no blocked stability, and whose `req_ids` intersect the
  requirements this work's files are already anchored to. Running `R-ORCH` over that would re-author
  requirements that exist and re-plan work that is planned.
- **`R-STEP` before `R-ORCH`**; the two cannot conflict, because D3 and D4 remove `R-STEP` for exactly the
  breadth and ordering conditions that make a run orchestration-shaped.
- **`R-ORCH` is total.** No predicate removes it. The ladder always terminates at exactly one rung, and
  `computeRoute` is total by construction — asserted by a property test over the fixture matrix.

**There are no ties, so there is no tie-breaking rule inside the classifier.** The only place a tie can
arise is the presentation gate's committee ballot, and §8.3 states what happens there.

### 3.5 Two narrowings, recorded because they are choices

1. **`kiwi-wave-master:160` is used at half strength.** Its own text lists `## Phase 1`, `## 1단계` *and*
   plain top-level section titles as natural wave boundaries. D4 uses only the ordered-marker half. The full
   reading fires on essentially every structured markdown document and would route a four-heading design memo
   away from the step rung and, in earlier proposals, straight into a four-wave run.
2. **Module count is not used.** An earlier proposal disqualified the step rung at `modules ≥ 2`, citing
   `kiwi-wave-master:165`. That line says the splitter is given existing modules and dependency direction so
   that a split does not cut **one module across several waves** — it says nothing about how many modules one
   unit may span, and `related_modules[]` has no schema-enforced granularity (`kiwi-srs/SKILL.md:291` is a
   JSON example, not a validator). D3's scope count replaces it: `scope` is a parsed record field
   (`src/core/query/filter.ts:28`) and `claim_step`'s single `touchesScope` (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:68`,
   `src/core/mutation/claim-step.ts:26`) is a real mechanical limit. The single-`--to-scope` promotion
   argument is not: promotion is one call per requirement id, so a step's requirements can be promoted
   into two scope documents.

### 3.6 Output

```ts
// src/core/orchestrator/route.ts — pure, no I/O
export type Rung = "R-STEP" | "R-PLAN" | "R-ORCH";
export type DisqualifierId = "D1" | "D2" | "D3" | "D4" | "D5" | "D6" | "D7" | "D8";

export interface RouteProbe {          // `parseRouteProbe`'s parsed projection of routing/probe.json
  mode: "sdd" | "vibe" | "wait" | "tdd";        // S1.mode
  modeSource: "mcp" | "cli" | "default-wait";   // S1.source
  planContractOk: boolean;                      // S2.contract_ok
  planRejectReason: string | null;              // S2.reject_reason
  planOpenTasks: number;                        // S2.open_tasks
  planReqIds: string[];                         // S2.req_ids (open Tasks)
  planTarget: string | null;                    // S2.target
  anchoredReqs: string[];                       // S3.anchored_reqs[]
  anchorCoverage: number;                       // S3c.anchor_coverage
  scopes: string[];                             // S4.scopes[]
  scopeReqIds: string[];                        // S4.scope_req_ids[]
  externalPaths: string[];                      // S5.external_paths[]
  ambiguities: number;                          // S6.ambiguities (§8.2 clause 3)
  orderedSections: number;                      // S7.ordered_sections
  linkedSubIssues: number;                      // S8.linked_sub_issues
  taskListGroups: number;                       // S8.task_list_groups
  declaredExistingReqEdit: boolean;             // S12.declared_existing_req_edit — D1's declared-judgment
                                                //   half over existing_srs_context.candidate_matches[]
  activeTarget: string | null;                  // S9.activeTarget
  blockedStability: string[];                   // S10.blocked_stability[]
  unreadable: Array<"S1"|"S2"|"S3"|"S3c"|"S4"|"S5"|"S6"|"S7"|"S8"|"S9"|"S10"|"S12">;  // S11.unreadable[]
}

export interface RouteDecision {
  rung: Rung;
  removed: Array<{ rung: Rung; by: DisqualifierId; observed: unknown }>;
  alternative: Rung | null;      // nearest surviving rung, for the gate's ballot
  decisive: { predicate: DisqualifierId; rung: Rung; observed: unknown } | null;  // §8.2
  recommended: boolean;          // §8.2
  withheld_because: string[];    // why `recommended` is false, when it is
}

export function computeRoute(p: RouteProbe, opts: { auto: boolean }): RouteDecision {
  const removed: RouteDecision["removed"] = [];
  const kill = (rung: Rung, by: DisqualifierId, observed: unknown) => removed.push({ rung, by, observed });

  if (p.anchoredReqs.length > 0 && p.anchorCoverage >= 0.2) kill("R-STEP", "D1", p.anchoredReqs);
  if (p.declaredExistingReqEdit)                            kill("R-STEP", "D1", "declared");
  if (p.externalPaths.length > 0)                           kill("R-STEP", "D2", p.externalPaths);
  if (p.scopes.length >= 2)                                 kill("R-STEP", "D3", p.scopes);
  if (p.orderedSections >= 2 || p.taskListGroups >= 1 || p.linkedSubIssues >= 2)
                                                            kill("R-STEP", "D4", p.orderedSections);
  if (!p.planContractOk)                                    kill("R-PLAN", "D5", p.planRejectReason);
  if (p.planOpenTasks === 0 || !coversThisWork(p))          kill("R-PLAN", "D6", coverageDiff(p));
  if (!p.activeTarget || p.blockedStability.length > 0)     kill("R-PLAN", "D7", p.blockedStability);
  for (const f of p.unreadable) for (const r of GATED_BY[f]) kill(r, "D8", f);

  const dead = new Set(removed.map((r) => r.rung));
  const order: Rung[] = ["R-PLAN", "R-STEP", "R-ORCH"];
  const surviving = order.filter((r) => !dead.has(r));
  const rung = surviving[0];                        // R-ORCH is never in `dead`
  return { rung, removed, alternative: surviving[1] ?? null,
           ...confidence(p, rung, removed, opts) };   // confidence() emits `decisive`,
                                                      // `recommended` and `withheld_because` (§8.2)
}
```

§3.2's `{producer, call, value, read_at}` envelope is the **on-disk** `probe.json` shape; `RouteProbe` is
`parseRouteProbe`'s parsed projection of it, which is why `unreadable[]` carries S-row ids while the parsed
properties are flat and untagged. That projection is what E48 (`05` §15.1), §10.1's totality property test and
IR-CLI-0t (§11) mean by *schema-valid*.

Two implementers given §3.2–§3.4 and this signature produce the same behaviour: every input has a named
producer and a named call, every predicate has a threshold in a stated unit, the selection order is fixed,
and the ladder is total.

## 4. The work-mode collision, resolved

### 4.1 The premise, dissolved first

`kiwi-pipeline` §2.8 (`kiwi-pipeline/SKILL.md:181-197`, FR-FLOW-039) routes on a **conjunction**: work-mode
is `tdd` **and** the request is step-scoped ⇒ `kiwi-tdd`; otherwise the five-stage sdd chain (`:193`, `:194`).
Only the first conjunct is operational. "step-scoped (단일 기능 / step 규모)" is given no test, no threshold
and no named input anywhere in §2.8 or in `_shared/kiwi/workmode-policy.md`.

So the new requirement is **not overriding a working mechanism**. It supplies the second conjunct that §2.8
declares and never defines. §3's disqualifier set *is* that definition: **step-scoped ≡ D1 ∧ D2 ∧ D3 ∧ D4 all
clear.** It only ever narrows the step rung, never widens it. The disqualifier set never widens it — but
§3.4's ordering narrows *execution* further still, and §4.3's third row records the one case where that
second narrowing departs from §2.8's consequent rather than conforming to it.

Note also that §2.8 excludes `--cycle` / `--from=` entries from its routing entirely (`:196`), and the
orchestrator's per-wave delegation calls `/kiwi-srs`, `/kiwi-planner` and `/kiwi-pm` individually (§5.3)
rather than entering `kiwi-pipeline --cycle`. §2.8 therefore never runs inside an orchestrator run, and there
is no live risk of two routers disagreeing mid-run. That is a fact about today's contract, and §11's §2.8 amendment
records it so a later change to `:196` does not silently create one.

### 4.2 The rule

> **The classifier decides scope. The persisted work-mode decides whether the step rung can execute. When
> they disagree, the disagreement is put on the gate as a user option — never resolved silently in either
> direction.**

The charter is explicit that the routing judgment "is the orchestrator's own judgment about the work, **not a
mode read from configuration**" (`00.charter.md:71`). A design in which `mode ≠ tdd` hard-removes the step
rung violates that sentence twice over: the default mode is `wait` (`workmode-policy.md:5`), which is also
the fail-open value (`:17`), so on any repository that never ran `set_work_mode` the user's own named route
would be structurally unreachable, decided entirely by configuration.

### 4.3 The four cases

| Case | Behaviour | Recorded |
|---|---|---|
| classifier = `R-STEP`, `S1.mode == "tdd"` | dispatch `/kiwi-tdd` | both conjuncts of §2.8 satisfied; noted in `route.lock.json` |
| classifier ≠ `R-STEP` **because D1–D4 removed the step rung**, `S1.mode == "tdd"` | route `R-PLAN` / `R-ORCH`. **This is conformance, not override** — `kiwi-pipeline/SKILL.md:194-195` already keeps body-scope REQ edits and large architecture changes on the sdd chain regardless of mode, and `kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:146` restates it from the consumer side: the wave cycle is body-scope, so tdd step routing does not apply | one **non-fatal WARN** naming the mode and the rung — the same shape and non-fatal semantics `workmode-policy.md:27` already uses for explicit-beats-derived |
| `R-STEP` survived (D1–D4 all clear) but `R-PLAN` won on §3.4's order, `S1.mode == "tdd"` | route `R-PLAN`. **This is an override of `kiwi-pipeline/SKILL.md:193`'s routing clause, not conformance** — the work is step-scoped under §4.1's own equivalence, and none of `:194`'s three exemptions (mode ≠ tdd / body-scope REQ edit / large architecture change) covers it. The ground is §3.4's own: a plan clearing D5–D7 already carries this work, so the run is plan-scoped in execution even where it is step-scoped in shape | one **non-fatal WARN** naming the mode, the rung **and the overridden clause**; `route-proposal` (§8.1) already carries `alternative = R-STEP` on its ballot, and §11's §2.8 amendment (§11, separate-requirements block) is where the override is written back |
| classifier = `R-STEP`, `S1.mode ≠ "tdd"` | gate **`route-step-requires-mode-switch`** (§4.5) | the gate's decision, its options and its resolution rule, in the lock |

**In every case, `tdd_policy` keeps flowing.** Mode `tdd` derives `tdd_policy = strict`
(`workmode-policy.md:9-16`), which `kiwi-planner` records into plan frontmatter and sidecar (`:34`) and
`kiwi-pm` WARNs on if contradicted (`:35`). A user in mode `tdd` whose work routes to `R-ORCH` still gets
strict TDD in every child Task. That is the concrete content of "the mode is not neutralised, only its
routing clause is", and it is `workmode-policy.md:36`'s own orthogonality statement read in the other
direction.

**The orchestrator never calls `set_work_mode` on its own authority.** The mode is a persisted, project-wide
user declaration stored in `docs/spec/steps/state.md` (`workmode-policy.md:5`). A router that mutates its own
precondition to make its preferred route legal has destroyed the only external check on itself. The call
exists and is reachable — but only as an option a human or a committee selects at the gate below.

### 4.4 `--auto` and the step rung

`kiwi-tdd` declares **no `critical_gates[]`** — verified by reading all 129 lines; the string does not occur.
By the shared safe default that means `--auto` is **inactive** for it (`_shared/kiwi/auto-option.md:23`,
`:237`), and `auto-option.md:317` states precisely what "inactive" means: **silent ignore**, not failure.
`kiwi-tdd` runs fine under `--auto`; it simply still stops at its own user gates. Two of its six
user-interaction points are already discharged by §5.1's dispatch preconditions and cannot fire on a
correctly dispatched run: the Phase 0 mode halt (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:64`) fires only when mode ≠ `tdd`,
which §5.1 forbids, and the missing-task-name query (`:37`) fires only when `<task>` is absent, which
§5.1 derives before invoking. What survives is the Phase 1 claim write-skew rejection (`:68`), the
Architecture-Decisions approval (`:79`, only when there is a substantive decision),
`EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` on promote (`:97`), and the Phase 7 completion gate (`:105`, only when a
non-clean compatibility edge survives) — the same four §5.1 names at its *Halted at one of its own
gates* outcome, minus the discharged mode halt.

So under `--auto`, selecting `R-STEP` produces a run that may stall at a gate no unattended mechanism
can clear — `kiwi-tdd` declares no `critical_gates[]`, so `--auto` cannot resolve any of them. The
design says so at the gate rather
than converting "this child ignores a flag" into "this child may never be selected":

**Gate `tdd-route-unattended`** — `business-decision`, not critical. It is evaluated **after**
`route-step-requires-mode-switch` (§4.5) and fires only when the rung is still `R-STEP` after that gate has
resolved, `--auto` is active, **and** that gate did not resolve to `switch-and-step`. When it resolved to
`stay-and-orchestrate` or `abort` the rung is no longer `R-STEP` and this gate is moot; when it resolved to
`switch-and-step` the dispatch was already chosen deliberately at a gate that showed its consequence, so
re-asking would discard the user's own selection. Two options: `proceed-step` (dispatch anyway; consequence line names the gates it may
stop at) and `orchestrate-instead` (route to `R-ORCH`), the latter carrying `recommended: true` so the
new `--auto` contract adopts it with no committee.

`kiwi-tdd` declaring `critical_gates[]` and joining the event enum is listed in §11's separate-requirements block as a **separate additive
requirement**, deliberately not folded into this one.

### 4.5 Gate `route-step-requires-mode-switch`

`business-decision`, **not** in `critical_gates[]`. Three options:

| id | Action | Consequence line shown to the user | `recommended` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `switch-and-step` | `set_work_mode(mode="tdd", activeTask=<task>)`, then dispatch `/kiwi-tdd` | "writes the persisted project-wide work-mode to `docs/spec/steps/state.md`; every later `kiwi-pipeline` §2.8 read in this repository returns `tdd` until you change it back; and under `--auto` the dispatched `kiwi-tdd` run may stop at its own gates, which `--auto` cannot clear (§4.4)" | no |
| `stay-and-orchestrate` | route to the nearest surviving rung (`R-PLAN` if it survived, else `R-ORCH`); mode untouched | "the work is step-shaped but the run proceeds on the sdd chain; `tdd_policy` still derives from your mode" | **yes** |
| `abort` | stop; write the probe and the run report | "no requirement, target, plan or work-mode is mutated; the run stops through `abort-run` (`05` §5.12), which leaves the integration branch `kiwi/orch/{run_id}/integration` and everything already written under `docs/research/{work}/` in place, releases the run lock, and names that state in the run report" | no |

Two consequences follow from the marker placement, and both are intended. Under `--auto` the gate resolves to
`stay-and-orchestrate` with zero deliberation — so while the marker stands, the mode is never mutated
unattended. Interactively the user
sees all three and can take the cheap rung deliberately. And when `S1.source == "default-wait"` — the mode
could not be read at all — the `recommended` marker on this gate is withheld entirely (§8.2), so an unread
setting can never buy the zero-deliberation path in either direction — the gate instead resolves on §8.3's
committee ballot over all three options, which may select `switch-and-step` on a 2-1 majority and write the
mode with no user present; a 1-1-1 has no majority and halts (§8.3).

### 4.6 Explicit user override of the rung — **WITHDRAWN**

Revision 4 declared that a user-named rung wins when no disqualifier removed it, grounded in
`workmode-policy.md:28`'s explicit-beats-derived convention, and that a contradicted override halts on
`route-override-contradicted` (critical). **Both are withdrawn.** No surface anywhere named a rung: `05` §14.1's
option table carries no rung option, `orchestrate route probe|freeze|show` take no rung argument, no probe
field holds one, and `computeRoute(p, opts: { auto: boolean })` has no parameter for one — while
`workmode-policy.md:28` enumerates *flag* precedences only. `00.charter.md:69-73` also requires routing to be
the orchestrator's own judgment rather than "a mode read from configuration", which leans against a
configured rung override. Explicit user preference is already served twice: §8.3's ballot lets the user (or,
under `--auto`, the committee) select `decision.alternative`, and a user who wants the wave engine can invoke
`kiwi-wave-master` directly, un-intercepted (FR-FLOW-0g). The gate `route-override-contradicted` is removed
from §8.1, from `05` §2.1's Phase 1 flow and from `05` §13. Building the surface instead would have reopened
§3.2's closed field set, `computeRoute`'s disqualifier-first purity and the lock schema for a capability the
charter never asked for.

## 5. The route table

### 5.1 `R-STEP` → `kiwi-tdd`

**Must exist before invoking**

- `routing/probe.json` and `routing/route.lock.json`, frozen, `rung = "R-STEP"`.
- A `<task>` name (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:37`): kebab, ≤40 chars, derived deterministically from
  `intent.json.summary` — the same derivation `kiwi-srs` uses for its run-id slug
  (`kiwi-srs/SKILL.md:158-160`).
- A work-overview paragraph (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:38` — the source of `intent.md`). This is
  `docs/research/{work}/01.intake.md`, already written at 1.c (`05.orchestrator-design.md §2.1 1.c`).
- `S1.mode == "tdd"` — either it already was, or `switch-and-step` was chosen at §4.5's gate.
- **No design document.** 1.d has not run and must not: `kiwi-tdd` authors its own SDS at Phase 2
  (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:72`, checklist at `:74-80`).

**Invocation**

```
Skill({ skill: "kiwi-tdd", args: "<task> [--auto] [--mini | --loops N] [--model <name>]" })
```

The `Skill` tool is the sanctioned parent→child mechanism (`kiwi-pipeline/SKILL.md:321-327`).

**Flags**

| Flag | Propagated | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `--mini` / `--loops N` | yes | `loop-option.md:48-54` propagation table. Acceptance is near-vacuous: `kiwi-tdd:26` says the round cap applies only to **regression-fix repetition**. Stated, not hidden |
| `--model <name>` | when the user specified it | `kiwi-pipeline:331` |
| `--auto` | passed for uniformity | **silently ignored by the child** (`auto-option.md:317`); the orchestrator must not read its lack of effect as an error. See §4.4 |
| `--max` | **no** | `kiwi-tdd` defines no `--max` semantics |
| pass-throughs (`--auto-integration`, `--auto-cost-warning`, `--force`, `--regression-baseline`) | **no** | they address `kiwi-pm` / `kiwi-coder` gates that do not exist on this path |

**While it runs.** The orchestrator holds no lock over the child's work and does not poll. It appends one
`dispatch-route` **intent** line before invoking and one **result** line after, per the write-ahead /
write-behind discipline (`05.orchestrator-design.md §4.3`).

**After it returns.** Three outcomes:

- *Promoted.* Record `outcome: "delegated-complete"` on the `dispatch-route` **result** line in

## 6. The engine-versus-skill ruling

> **`R-ORCH` drives the shared wave engine directly. `kiwi-orchestrator` never invokes `kiwi-wave-master`.**

Consistent with D1, which settles coexistence over an extracted engine rather than replacement
(`00.charter.md:108`), and with the charter's own reading that "the honest form of 'route to wave-master' may
be 'drive the shared wave engine directly'" (`:84-86`).

Four grounds. A fifth argument that earlier drafts used is dropped, and the drop is recorded.

1. **After D1, invoking the sibling is the duplicated path.** The engine moves into
   `_shared/kiwi/{wave-decomposition,verify-loop,isolation-and-integration,run-ledger}.md`
   (`05.orchestrator-design.md §9.1`) and the artifact root becomes a parameter precisely so one
   implementation serves two callers (`05.orchestrator-design.md §9.1`). Calling a thin caller from a caller to reach modules the
   orchestrator already references is the architectural debt `00.charter.md:100` forbids.
2. **The input contract does not fit.** `kiwi-wave-master` does not author requirements or code (`:10`) and
   requires an input document it did not write, halting without one (`:69`; gate `decomposition-input-missing`
   at `:47`, whose stated reason is that "the input document is not something the committee invents by
   inference"). The orchestrator arrives at Phase 2 holding a design **it authored and froze**
   (`05.orchestrator-design.md §2.1 1.d–1.e`). Handing that back as a "required input" makes wave-master re-derive
   `design_items` from it (`:175`), and any drift between the two derivations means the run is verified
   against a denominator different from the one it was planned on — which is exactly what `:299`'s
   "the verifier does not compute its own denominator" rule exists to prevent.
3. **Run-scoped pins collide.** wave-master's preflight pins the journal root **before** any journal read
   (`:129`) and captures the full regression suite **once** and pins it run-wide (`:150`), and it accepts no
   `--regression-baseline` input — it only forwards one to children (`:528`). A nested child therefore
   necessarily re-measures the suite and overwrites the parent's P.4 pin
   (`05.orchestrator-design.md §2.1 P.4`), which is the precise mechanism `:150` exists to stop: a later wave's
   own failures being promoted into the next wave's baseline.
4. **Journal ownership.** `waves.jsonl` resume is run-scoped and reads only events matching the current
   `run_id` (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:481`, `:483`), and `05.orchestrator-design.md §4.2` records that
   `waves-event.md` has **no producer discriminator** today — the reason v1.4.0 adds `engine`. A spawned
   wave-master mints its own `run_id` and writes lines under a second engine into one file for one logical
   run, so the discriminator the extension adds stops discriminating.

**Dropped: the self-recursion argument.** An earlier draft claimed a nested wave-master would trip
`kiwi-pipeline`'s self-recursion guard. It would not: `kiwi-pipeline/SKILL.md:28` (§0.5) is about
`kiwi-pipeline` specifically — "본 스킬의 `next_hint` 가 `kiwi-pipeline` 인 경우 … 직전 직전 이벤트도
`kiwi-pipeline` 이면 ERROR" — not about two consecutive same-skill events in general. The conclusion stands
on grounds 1–4; that argument does not support it.

**What stays a skill invocation, and the rule behind the asymmetry.**

> **Invoke the skill when the skill is a leaf, or a chain the orchestrator does not own. Drive the engine
> when the skill is a shell whose outer layer the orchestrator has already built.**

`kiwi-tdd` is a leaf: it delegates to nothing — its 129 lines contain no skill invocation, only MCP/CLI tool
calls (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:120-129`) — and its value is the red/green discipline plus the promote path, which
is not extractable machinery. `kiwi-pm` is a chain the orchestrator does not own: its value is per-Task
isolation in a fresh `Agent` with no main-session context accumulation, which is why it forbids itself the
`Skill` tool (`kiwi-pm/SKILL.md:34`). Re-implementing either would be duplication in the opposite direction,
and D2 says compose (`00.charter.md:109`).

**`kiwi-wave-master` remains reachable and unchanged as a user-facing entry.** It has its own trigger
vocabulary (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:3`). If the user names it, the orchestrator does not intercept, does
not classify and does not start a run — it reports that the request names a sibling skill and stops.

## 7. Misjudgement recovery

### 7.1 The rule

> **Escalation is one-way — `R-STEP → R-ORCH` and `R-PLAN → R-ORCH`. De-escalation is refused. Gating is a
> function of what has already landed, not of the transition.**

There is no `R-STEP → R-PLAN` edge (a step has no plan) and no `R-PLAN → R-STEP` edge (a plan is body-scope by
construction). Both cheap rungs escalate to the same place, which is why the carry manifests below share a
destination and a sealing mechanism.

De-escalation is refused because the journals do not permit it: `complete` is append-only with no
`complete → failed` edge (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:253`) and §0.6 resume skips completed waves forever
(`:23`). The exit is `abort-run` (`05.orchestrator-design.md §4.4`), which is terminal, names the repository
state the user is left holding, and writes it to the run report.

### 7.2 E1 · `R-STEP → R-ORCH`

**Triggers, all owned by machinery `kiwi-tdd` already runs and already stops at:**

| Trigger | When it is observable | Cost at that moment |
|---|---|---|
| (a) the SDS approaches the 200-line cap — `kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:78` checklist item 5, normative at `docs/rule/SDS-MD-Rules-v2.5.0.md:35`, and named as the large-change signal at `:114` | Phase 2, **before the first red test** | near zero — `kiwi-tdd` commits tests first (`:84`), so nothing is committed |
| (b) an existing body-REQ edit is detected (`:113`), or `promote_step_requirement` returns `MUTATION_DENIED` | Phase 6, after green | the implementation is already written |

**Trigger (b) firing is a classifier defect, not a route failure.** D1 exists to prevent it. When it fires,
`routing/misroute-{n}.json` must name the file whose anchor should have hit and the observed `anchor_coverage`
— those records are the only evidence from which D1's recall is ever measured.

**Explicitly not triggers:** `EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` on promote (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:97`) and
`COMPLETION_GATE_BLOCKED` on merge (`:105`). Those are defects in the run — fill the evidence, resolve the
compatibility closure, retry. Escalating on a quality failure lets a stuck run climb until it runs out of rungs.

**Carry manifest**

| Trigger | Carries | Lost | Sealed |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | `docs/spec/steps/<task>/design.md` and `intent.md` become `0N.*.md` research inputs to 1.d (`05.orchestrator-design.md §2.1 1.c`), and then `/kiwi-srs --research-doc` per wave | the `claim_step` round trip and the `scaffold_step` stubs | nothing — nothing landed |
| (b) | as (a), plus the merged tests and implementation stay on the integration branch | one planner run for the remainder | the completed work is recorded in the `R-ORCH` design baseline as `out_of_scope` with `exclusion_class = "already-implemented"` (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:171`; closed enum at `_shared/kiwi/waves-event.md:120`) |

**The green implementation is never re-planned as a Task.** `kiwi-coder` runs every code Task through a
mandatory red confirmation — §0.1 at `kiwi-coder/SKILL.md:18` fixes the order "테스트 작성 → … → red 실패
확인 → 구현 → green 확인" and forbids bypass, and "red 미발생" is a CRITICAL finding. Carrying green tests over
green implementation would fail every carried Task. The `already-implemented` seal is the correct disposition
and it is a value the closed enum already carries for exactly this case. The seal fires
`out-of-scope-user-consent` (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:51`, `:205`) **even under `--auto`**, so no escalation
that discards work from a denominator is silent.

**Lease hygiene, verified rather than assumed.** On escalation the orchestrator calls
`update_step_state(<task>, "abandoned")`. `ALLOWED_STATUSES` is the flat set
`["active","merging","merged","abandoned"]` with no transition graph
(`src/core/mutation/update-step-state.ts:41`), the completion gate applies only to `merged` (`:97`,
`COMPLETION_GATE_BLOCKED` at `:119`), and `claim_step`'s conflict screen counts only `active`/`merging` rows
(`src/core/mutation/claim-step.ts:45`). So abandoning genuinely releases the claim and cannot itself be
blocked. `update_step_state(merged)` is **not** called — the step's requirement will be authored at body scope
by `/kiwi-srs` instead.

- `kiwi-pm` returns `FAILED` naming missing prior work (§0.G4; `task-failure-escalation` is a declared critical

- the lifecycle gate stops on an empty active target (`kiwi-pm/SKILL.md:562`, pseudocode `:586-587`, gate

**Carry manifest.** The plan and sidecar carry as **evidence**, not as a plan to re-run. Completed Tasks are

in E1(b). The un-executed remainder is re-planned by `/kiwi-planner` inside the wave — **one planner run**,
the admitted cost of this edge.

### 7.4 The one downgrade, and where it is legal

Over-routing to `R-ORCH` is the expensive error and `R-ORCH` is the total default, so the design owes a
mitigation. It is not a fourth rung; it is a second, better-informed look at the last moment it is free.

**Gate `route-downgrade-available`** — `business-decision`, not critical — fires at the **end of Phase 2**
alongside `05`'s refusal-floor evaluation (`05.orchestrator-design.md §1.4`), when all four hold:

- the decomposition returned `N == 1`;
- the wave's `design_items` count is at or below the step threshold recorded in the probe;
- no disqualifier removed `R-STEP` at 1.c′ (so the cheap rung was legal all along, and the run is at
  `R-ORCH` for some reason other than an `R-STEP` disqualifier — the mode gate (§4.5),
  `tdd-route-unattended` (§4.4), or a user or committee override of an `R-STEP` proposal at
  `route-proposal` (§8.3), where `decision.alternative == R-ORCH` was selected). Re-offering the cheap
  rung to a user who already declined it at 1.c′ is deliberate: 2.e carries `N == 1` and the
  `design_items` count, which the 1.c′ ballot did not, so this is new evidence rather than §4.4's
  re-ask of a settled question;
- no SRS mutation on this run has moved a requirement's `status` or `stability`. This is §7.5's
  **second** disjunct and deliberately not its first: §2.3 lands two `commit-run-artifacts` commits on
  the integration branch on every run, so the commit disjunct would make the condition always false.
  A run that reached `R-ORCH` at 1.c′ trivially satisfies it; a run that arrived here by §7.3 E2 (or
  §7.2 E1(b)) may already carry `status` mutations from its `R-PLAN` child (§1's `R-PLAN` row makes
  the first child `kiwi-coder` `update_status(…, "in_progress")` the run's first irreversible act),
  and this condition excludes exactly that case.

Options: `continue-orchestrated` (**recommended**) and `downgrade-to-step`.

**What `downgrade-to-step` actually does, because §5.1's dispatch preconditions are not bypassed.**
(i) When `S1.mode ≠ "tdd"` — the mode-gate branch, since a mode-gate
`stay-and-orchestrate` leaves the mode untouched (§4.5) — selecting it **re-raises**
`route-step-requires-mode-switch` (§4.5), and only `switch-and-step` proceeds; declining leaves the run
orchestrated. §5.1's mode precondition is therefore never bypassed, and §4.4's discharge of `kiwi-tdd`'s
Phase 0 halt holds. (ii) `freeze-route` re-writes `route.lock.json` with `rung = "R-STEP"` under the
verb `downgrade-route` (§9.4) and the append-new-artifact rule, and the new digest re-enters
`invariant_digest`, so §9.5's read-never-recompute resume returns the **downgraded** rung rather than
silently re-entering the orchestrated ladder. (iii) `design/00.design.md` and the decomposition stay on
disk as sunk artifacts, named in the run report and **not** passed to `kiwi-tdd`; §5.1's *"no design
document"* bullet is an entry-order rule for the 1.c′ dispatch (§2.2 reason 2 — the design is worthless
to `R-STEP`, not incompatible with it), not a bar on this transition.

This is legal at exactly this
point and nowhere later, and that is what condition 4 above evaluates rather than assumes. On a run
that reached `R-ORCH` at 1.c′, Phase 3.b is the run's first SRS mutation
(`05.orchestrator-design.md §1.4`): nothing has been registered, so nothing must be retracted. A run
that arrived by §7.3 E2 (or §7.2 E1(b)) may already carry `status` mutations from its `R-PLAN` child,
which condition 4 excludes. After 3.b the
gate does not exist and `route-deescalation-refused` applies.

The marker is on `continue-orchestrated` so `--auto` never silently downgrades a run the user launched
unattended; the cheap path stays a deliberate human choice.

### 7.5 Escalation gating

| Gate | Fires when | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `route-escalation-after-landed-state` | the escalation follows **either** a commit on the integration branch **or** an SRS mutation that moved a requirement's `status` **or** its `stability` | **critical**, in `critical_gates[]` |
| — | E1 trigger (a), detected before the first red test — the common and intended case | free and **ungated** |

The `stability` clause is load-bearing and was wrong in an earlier draft that named only `update_status`:
`/kiwi-srs-feasibility` mutates `stability` target-wide, not `status`, so a condition testing only `status`
classifies a post-feasibility escalation as free. Naming the *condition* rather than the *transition* is what
keeps unattended runs alive — the cheap escalation does not halt, and the expensive one always does. Gating a
free escalation would teach the agent to hide the trigger.

**Why `route-proposal` is not critical.** It fires on **every** run. `critical_gates[]` membership means halt
regardless of `--auto` (`auto-option.md:188`; the committee never overrides a critical halt, `:156`), so

### 8.2 The `recommended` marker

The contract in force is `00.charter.md:30-35` as amended by `08.scope-and-frame-decisions.md:46-52`:

> A gate offering a **recommended** option adopts it immediately, with no committee.
> Otherwise a committee of 3 — 5 under `--max` — decides by **simple majority**.
> **If no majority exists, the gate escalates to `critical` and halts for the user.** (No tie rung: `08` §2, 5-0.)
> Gates in `critical_gates[]` halt regardless of `--auto`.

`route-proposal` carries `recommended: true` **iff all of**:

1. `probe.unreadable == []` — D8 did not fire;
2. `decision.decisive` is non-`null` **and** either has margin ≥ 1 in its own unit or is a boolean
   corroborated by at least one other predicate that also fired;
3. `S6.ambiguities == 0` after 1.c's QnA — its empty-array case is already an operative predicate in the suite
   (`kiwi-srs/SKILL.md:329`);
4. when `rung == "R-STEP"`: `S1.source ≠ "default-wait"` — an unread mode never buys the fast path;
5. when `rung == "R-STEP"` and D1 cleared it by measurement: `anchor_coverage ≥ 0.2`.

**`decisive`, defined so clause 2 is computable.** No predicate selects a rung (§3.3), so "the decisive
predicate" cannot mean one that acted on the selected rung. It is **the removal of the rung nearest above the
selected one in the fixed order `R-PLAN → R-STEP → R-ORCH`** — the removal that made the selected rung
first-surviving. When several predicates removed that rung, the lowest D-id wins. When no rung above the
selected one was removed — `R-PLAN` selected, or `removed[] == []` — `decisive` is `null`, and **clause 2
fails**: a `null` decisive withholds the marker rather than passing vacuously, because a run with nothing
removed has no measured discrimination to fast-path on. That polarity is a decision, not an inference. Because
`R-PLAN` is first in the fixed order, an `R-PLAN` selection always yields `decisive == null`, so the marker is
withheld on **every** `R-PLAN` selection — including one where D6 cleared it on the substitute-link branch, which
therefore needs no clause of its own (revision 5's clause 6 was deleted as unreachable, not relaxed).

**Margin, per predicate, in its own unit.** D3: `|S4| − 2`. D4: `max(S7.ordered_sections − 2,
S8.linked_sub_issues − 2, S8.task_list_groups − 1)`. A count predicate observed **exactly at** its threshold
has margin **0**, so it does not satisfy clause 2's numeric branch — that is the clause's whole point, and it
is why §9.2's worked lock withholds the marker on a two-scope observation. D1, D2, D5, D6, D7 and D8 are
boolean or set-non-empty and have no numeric margin; they take the corroboration branch instead.

Otherwise the marker is withheld and `withheld_because[]` names which clause failed. **`recommended` is a
pure function of the probe**, so a reviewer can re-run `computeRoute` over the recorded `probe.json` and get
the same boolean — which is what makes the zero-deliberation path auditable at all.

Per `07.auto-contract-decisions.md:153-155`, the marker is a **structured field** on the one structured
options schema that exists, and an unparsed prose `(권장)` confers nothing. `07:148-151` records why: two of
the three existing informal `(권장)` labels annotate a **HALT** option.

**This gate is the first declared consumer of the new marker** (`07:157-159` notes it otherwise ships unused),
which is the concrete reason D4 (`00.charter.md:111`) ships the `--auto` change alongside the skill.

### 8.3 The ballot, the committee input, and the audit row

**Ballot shape.** The selected rung plus `decision.alternative` — the nearest surviving rung — or, when
`alternative` is `null`, the selected rung plus `abort`. Two options, three members: a tie is arithmetically
impossible. The residual halt is `07.auto-contract-decisions.md:173-179`'s degraded quorum — one member
fails, two remain, 1-1 has no majority — which under `08` §2 escalates to `critical` and halts.
`route-step-requires-mode-switch` has three options and a 1-1-1 **is** possible there; it halts, which is
correct for a gate whose first option mutates persisted project state.

**`abort` at this gate.** Same option as §4.5's row: stop; the run ends through `abort-run`
(`05.orchestrator-design.md` §4.4, §5.12). Consequence line: *"no repository state is changed."* It resolves
**before** `freeze-route`, so **no `routing/route-gate.json` and no `route.lock.json` are written** —
`selected ∈ {"proposed", "alternative"}` (below) indexes rung options only and is never asked to name
`abort`. `probe.json` and `00.routing.md` are already on disk (§9.1) and are named in the run report; in
phase 1 `abort-run` reaps nothing (`05` §5.12 step 1 is a no-op there).

**Committee input carries facts, never the proposal.** `auto-option.md:38-49` forbids passing the main
session's tentative decision (`:49`, 전달 금지). Members receive `gate_id`, `severity`, `options[]`, the
**probe table** and the **removal table**. The prose file `routing/00.routing.md` is what the *user* reads;
the committee reads evidence. A committee handed a recommendation to review is a rubber stamp with three seats.

**Confidence under a split vote** is the **minimum within the winning bloc**, after the `auto-option.md`
§4.1.1 adjustments — `07` §3a, carried verbatim as a condition of Q2. §4.1.2's confidence-spread rung is
folded into §4.1.1's threshold mechanism with no re-vote on spread alone (`07` §4.1).

**Audit.** A fast-pathed decision writes
`{"rule": "recommended-fastpath", "committee_size": 0, "marked_by": "routing/route.lock.json#gate.recommended"}`
into `docs/analysis/{skill-run-id}/auto_decisions.json` (`07` §4.5; schema location `auto-option.md:334`).
Without it, the decisions that received **no** deliberation would be the ones leaving **no** evidence.

**Interactive mode.** `AskUserQuestion` with the same options, plus the prose file already on disk. If the
user picks the alternative, the lock records `rung`, `proposed_rung`, `overridden_by: "user"` — and
**`computeRoute` is not re-run**. A classifier that re-runs and agrees with the user has stopped measuring.

**Committee override.** `overridden_by` is `"user" | "committee" | null`. When the committee selects the
alternative under `--auto`, the lock records `rung` (the alternative), `proposed_rung` (the classifier's
selection), `overridden_by: "committee"`, and the ballot's resolution row — `rule`, `committee_size` and
the vote split — in `gates[].resolution`. The same no-re-run rule applies: `computeRoute` is not re-run
after either override. Both options were computed survivors (§3.1), so an override changes which of the
classifier's own outputs executes and never introduces a rung the classifier did not produce.

**How the resolution reaches the lock: `routing/route-gate.json` (§9.1).** `RouteDecision` (§3.6) is a
closed field set and carries none of `proposed_rung`, `overridden_by` or `gates[].resolution`, so
`freezeRoute` as typed cannot write a conformant lock on either override branch. The orchestrator therefore
writes one small record **at** the gate, before `freeze-route`, and passes it as `freezeRoute`'s third
argument (§10.1):

```json
{
  "schema_version": "1.0.0",
  "probe_digest": "sha256:…",
  "gates": [
    { "gate_id": "route-proposal", "severity": "business-decision",
      "selected": "alternative",
      "decided_by": "committee",
      "resolution": { "rule": "majority", "committee_size": 3, "split": "2-1" } }
  ]
}
```

Four rules fix its shape and keep it inside every existing ruling:

1. **It names a *selection*, never a rung.** `selected ∈ {"proposed", "alternative"}` indexes the two
   ballot options this gate already had. `freezeRoute` resolves it against `decision`: `proposed_rung` is
   always `decision.rung`, and `rung` is `decision.rung` or `decision.alternative`. §4.6's withdrawal is
   therefore untouched — `orchestrate route probe|freeze|show` still take **no rung argument**, no surface
   names a rung, and the *never introduces a rung the classifier did not produce* guarantee above holds by
   construction rather than by discipline.
2. **`overridden_by` is derived, not carried:** `null` when `selected == "proposed"`, otherwise
   `decided_by` (`"user"` | `"committee"`), matching §9.2's enum and `05` AA-06.
3. **`split` is recorded at the gate site, not returned by the kernel.** `decideAutoGate`'s declared return
   is `{action, memberCount, rule, reason}` (`05` §10.1) and has no `split`; the tally is the orchestrator's
   own, held when it convened the committee. The kernel is **not** widened for this.
4. **It is an input, not a pinned artifact.** Only `route.lock.json` joins `frozen` (§9.3). A redo of
   `freeze-route` re-reads this record and reproduces the same lock, so the digest matches and the verb is
   the no-op `05` §4.4 promises; a record edited after the freeze surfaces as `run-invariant-drift` at that
   redo, which is the correct detection rather than a silent second rung.

On the fast path and on an un-overridden gate the record still exists — `selected: "proposed"`,
`resolution` carrying `{"rule": "recommended-fastpath", "committee_size": 0, "marked_by": …}` — so
`freeze-route` has exactly one input shape on every branch.

`coverage_residual[]` is a `{req_id, reason, owner}` array written on the `R-PLAN` `dispatch-route` **result**

no analogue on a delegated rung (§13 A9).

journal is append-only and outside every digest, and it already carries a `residual` array of this shape
(`05` §4.2). It is what the `plan-coverage-unclosed` gate reads; an absent or empty array on any other rung is
the normal state.

### 9.3 It joins `frozen`

`05.orchestrator-design.md §4.1`'s `frozen` block gains

```json
"route": { "rung": "R-ORCH", "lock": "…/routing/route.lock.json@sha256:…", "probe_digest": "sha256:…" }
```

so `invariant_digest` (`05.orchestrator-design.md §4.1`) covers it. This is exactly the repair

sitting outside the digest, with no enforcement anywhere. A resumed session that recomputed a different rung
now raises `run-invariant-drift` (critical) rather than silently switching ladders mid-run.

### 9.4 Journal verbs

Five additions to the closed verb enum (`05.orchestrator-design.md §4.4`); an out-of-enum verb is a
hard stop on resume (`05.orchestrator-design.md §4.4`).

| verb | recovery class | interrupted-recovery rule |
|---|---|---|
| `probe-route` | **idempotent-by-key** | keyed on `probe.json`. **Not `pure-reauthor`:** several probe fields (S5, S6) are subagent-derived and are not reproducible across a compaction, so a redo could produce a different rung. Recovery reads the persisted probe; it does not re-judge. An `intent` with no `result` means the probe file may be partial — validate it against the schema, and re-run only the fields marked unreadable |
| `freeze-route` | idempotent-by-key | content-addressed; a redo is a no-op when the digest matches (mirrors `freeze-design`, `05` §4.4) |
| `dispatch-route` | **externally-visible** | the child mutates. Inspect the child's own `pipeline.jsonl` event, then MCP `list_steps` (`R-STEP`) or `workflow_plan_status` (`R-PLAN`), before re-entering. Never re-dispatch a possibly-running child |
| `escalate-route` | **externally-visible** | rewrites the lock, may seal work as `out_of_scope`, may re-enter `/kiwi-srs`. Inspect `list_requirements --target …` and the latest `freeze-route` result first |
| `downgrade-route` | **externally-visible** | the 2.e `downgrade-to-step` resolution only (§7.4). It writes a **new** `route.lock.json` per `05` §4.7's append-new-artifact rule rather than editing the frozen one, moves `frozen.route` to the new lock and recomputes `invariant_digest`, then hands off through `dispatch-route`. Recovery: read the latest journalled route lock — the rung is read, never recomputed — and inspect the child's `pipeline.jsonl` before re-entering. `escalate-route` is **not** widened to cover it: escalation is one-way (§7.1) |

### 9.5 How a resumed session reads it

1. Read the resume card; validate `invariant_digest` over `frozen`.
2. Read `frozen.route.rung`. **The rung is read, never recomputed.**
3. Verify `frozen.route.probe_digest` against `probe.json` on disk. Mismatch ⇒ `run-invariant-drift` (critical).
4. Resolve the last line for the `(verb, wave, lane)` key per `05.orchestrator-design.md §4.3`'s
   invariant; a `dispatch-route` **intent** with no **result** raises `interrupted-external-action`.

`computeRoute` runs **exactly once per run**. A resumed session has no conversation and no investigators; if
the rung were not reconstructible from `route.lock.json` alone it would not be reconstructible at all — the
same discipline `kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:193` imposes on the design baseline for the same reason.

### 10.2 Prose — `skills/{claude,codex,etc}/kiwi-orchestrator/SKILL.md`

- how the subagent-derived probe fields are gathered: S5, S6 and S12 (`declared_existing_req_edit`,
  read from `existing_srs_context.json`). These require reading intent; the tool checks only that the
  values were recorded in `probe.json`.
  The remaining fields need **no** unit and are named here so §10's two halves are exhaustive over the
  probe: S1 and S9 are direct tool reads, S8 is `gh issue view` output, and S10 is a stability filter
  over the record set S3c already read.
- the gate option text and consequence lines (§8.3, §4.5, §4.4, §7.4).
- the escalation triggers that are judgments made **inside a child skill** — the SDS cap check is a checklist
  item an agent applies (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:78`), not a tool output. Stated honestly: the orchestrator learns
  of trigger (a) only if the child reports it, so E1's ledger is only as complete as `kiwi-tdd`'s
  self-reporting.

### 10.3 How the prose is tested, and what that test does not prove

Raw-text presence plus proximity assertions across the three variants, the harness pattern already in use for
wave-verify content. `01.wave-master-current-contract.md:470-479` measures exactly what that buys: nothing in
this repository executes the wave cycle, and the wave contract is asserted entirely by text presence, so a
green suite proves the sentence exists in three variants and is not behavioural verification. Making
`computeRoute` a pure function **narrows** the untested surface — the classifier itself becomes genuinely
testable — it does not close it.

## 12. Rejected alternatives — do not re-propose

1. **Free-text `search_requirements` union over `intent.json.key_entities[]` as the overlap probe.**
   `searchRequirementRecords` is a literal case-insensitive `indexOf` over ten fields including `traceLinks`
   and `verificationEvidence` (`src/core/query/discovery.ts:138-151`, `:161-173`), and the probe took the
   union over a term list — a common token saturates (the step rung is removed on almost every input, and the
   plan rung with it), a novel vocabulary empties (an unrelated stale plan passes the subset test vacuously).
   Replaced by the `traceReference` exact-match filter (`src/core/query/filter.ts:35`).

2. **Wave count `W` as a routing input.** The splitter's non-structured branch returns 3–8 sub-goals
   (`kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:161`) and cannot return one, so `W == 1` is unreachable for unstructured input
   and every unstructured design routes to the wave shell. `W` is a Phase 2 measurement.

3. **`top_level_sections ≥ 2` (any heading) as the wave-entry test.** `kiwi-wave-master:160` names plain
   top-level section titles, so a five-heading research memo would register five SRS targets. Only the
   ordered-marker half of `:160` is used, and the narrowing is recorded (§3.5).

4. **`modules ≥ 2` as a step disqualifier.** Its cited basis (`:165`) forbids cutting one module *across*
   waves and says nothing about how many modules one unit may span; `related_modules[]` has no
   schema-enforced granularity. Replaced by `|scopes| ≥ 2`, grounded in `claim_step`'s single
   `--touches-scope` (`kiwi-tdd/SKILL.md:68`).

5. **`mode ≠ tdd` as a hard disqualifier for the step rung.** `00.charter.md:71` requires the routing to be
   the orchestrator's own judgment "not a mode read from configuration", and the default mode is `wait`
   (`workmode-policy.md:5`), so a hard disqualifier makes the user's own named route unreachable on every
   repository that never ran `set_work_mode`. Replaced by §4.5's gate.

6. **`--auto` as a hard disqualifier for the step rung.** `--auto` on a skill that declares no
   `critical_gates[]` is a **silent ignore** (`auto-option.md:317`), not a failure. Converting "this child
   ignores a flag" into "this child may never be selected" disables the cheapest rung in exactly the mode the
   user chose to reduce supervision. Replaced by §4.4's gate.

7. **A cross-Phase `depends_on_task` edge as the escalation trigger.** The field is documented as
   **same-phase** ordering (`kiwi-planner/SKILL.md:359`, `:362`, and `:757`'s own comment "동일 phase 내
   선후"); cross-phase dependency is `Phase.depends_on` (`:358`, `:737`), which is populated on essentially
   every multi-phase plan and would escalate near-universally.

8. **A fourth "single wave" rung distinct from the wave shell.** It is the same engine with `N == 1`, and
   `05.orchestrator-design.md §1.4`'s refusal floor already handles the degradation at the end of Phase 2
   where the measurement exists. §7.4's downgrade gate is the whole of what that rung would have bought.

9. **Making `route-proposal` critical.** It fires on every run, so it would stall every unattended run at its
   first decision — `08.scope-and-frame-decisions.md:60-64` sustained exactly this objection.

10. **Writing a synthetic `complete` line to retrofit already-merged work as wave-1.**
    `_shared/kiwi/waves-event.md:135` invalidates any `complete` lacking a preceding passing wave-verify
    record in the same run, and resume then reads that wave as incomplete. The `already-implemented`
    `exclusion_class` seal is used instead.

11. **Inheriting `kiwi-wave-master`'s `critical_gates[]` by rule rather than declaring them.**
    `kiwi-pm/SKILL.md:101`'s inheritance rule requires a running child bubbling a `gate_id` from its own
    table; `_shared` modules are not children and carry no gate declarations (`auto-option.md:226-237`,
    `:239-252`). Undeclared gates fall to `business-decision` (`auto-option.md:190`) and a committee approves
    them under `--auto` — the precise failure `kiwi-wave-master/SKILL.md:34` warns about.

12. **Nesting `kiwi-wave-master` inside an orchestrator run.** §6 grounds 1–4. Note that the *self-recursion*
    argument for this rejection does not hold — `kiwi-pipeline/SKILL.md:28` guards against `kiwi-pipeline`
    specifically — and the ruling does not rest on it.
