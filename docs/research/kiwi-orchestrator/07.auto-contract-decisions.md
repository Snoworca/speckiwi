## 1. The three decisions

| | Question | Decision | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | What happens to `--max`? | **`max-5-majority`** — `--max` convenes 5 and decides by majority. No escalation ladder. | 5-0 unanimous |
| Q2 | Promote the three irreversible / externally visible gates to `critical_gates[]`? | **`leave-all-three`** — leave them to the committee. **Conditional; see §3.** | 5-0 unanimous |
| Q3 | What happens on a 1-1-1 tie? | ~~**`tie-escalate-to-5`** — add two members once, then the lead member breaks a persisting tie.~~ **SUPERSEDED by `08` §2 (5-0): no rung; the gate escalates to `critical` and halts.** | 3-2 plurality |

The resulting contract, stated in full:

> A gate offering a **recommended** option adopts it immediately, with no committee.
> Otherwise a committee of 3 members — 5 under `--max` — decides by simple majority.
> If no majority exists, add two members once and re-vote; a still-unresolved tie breaks on the lead
> member's ranking.
> Gates in `critical_gates[]` halt for the user regardless of `--auto`.

Note what the escalation trigger is now. It is **"no majority exists"**, not "not unanimous". That is
strictly narrower, and it fires only in the case the user's own rule cannot resolve at all. A 2-1
split decides immediately, which is what the user asked for.

## 2. A premise in the question was wrong

Q1 was posed on the assumption that removing the 3 → 5 escalation removes what feeds the `--max`
5 → 7 escalation. That is false: `auto-option.md:32` seeds `--auto --max` with a 5-member committee
directly, so `max-keep-5-to-7` was structurally coherent and had to be rejected on other grounds —
principally that it leaves the two modes following different rules.

The impact study `04.auto-recommended-fastpath-impact.md` repeats the same error at its §3.2 and §4.3
("the `--max` ladder becomes a two-rung 5 → 7 with no feeder"). Whoever authors the SRS from that
study should not carry the error into the requirement text.

## 3. Q2 is conditional — do not implement it unconditionally

Leaving the three irreversible gates to a 2-1 majority was unanimous, but two members made their
vote explicitly conditional, and one stated that without the condition their vote flips to
`promote-all-three`. The conditions are:

**3a. The confidence input under a split vote must be pinned.** `auto-option.md:186` gates
`business-decision` on confidence ≥ 0.7. Under unanimity "the confidence" was unambiguous. Under a
2-1 it is undefined — the winning bloc's mean, the minimum across all members, or the lead's?
**Decision: the minimum confidence within the winning bloc, after the §4.1.1 adjustments.** This is
the conservative reading; it keeps §4.1.1's ×0.7 penalty for an empty `side_effects[]` on a mutation
gate biting on exactly the gates Q2 leaves committee-decided; and it means a 2-1 carried by one
confident and one hesitant member escalates to critical rather than shipping.

**3b. Q3's escalation must exist.** A 1-1-1 at a `Closes #N` or an `apply-selected` gate settled by
one member alone is the combination the committee would not accept. Q2 and Q3 are a package.

One correction the committee made to the impact study, which materially weakened the case for
promoting one of the three gates: `kiwi-commit-auto-pr` §14.9 *does* declare "기존 PR 본문 보존 vs
`--update-pr-body`" a committee-decided `business-decision` gate (`SKILL.md:849`), exactly as `04` §4
records — but the committee's answer cannot reach the destructive path. The skill branches on the
presence of the `--update-pr-body` flag (`SKILL.md:270-271`), skips body authoring entirely when absent
(`:292`), makes preservation the default with destructive update "only when `--update-pr-body` is
specified" (`:556`), and blocks it in child mode unless explicitly propagated (`:442`). The declared gate is therefore **inert, not absent**: the destructive path is user-flag-gated one layer below `critical_gates[]`.

Note also that Q2 decided a **named sample**, not a boundary. `auto-option.md:190` classifies any gate
with no declared severity as `business-decision`, so the population moving to 2-1 is open-ended by
design. The boundary is `critical_gates[]` plus the §4.1.1 confidence rule — and §4.1.1 must be
carried into the rewrite **verbatim**, because it is the only thing in the contract sensitive to
irreversibility rather than to vote counts.

## 4. The edits nobody asked about — all mandatory

Every member independently found these. Each one makes the contract self-contradictory if skipped.

### 4.1 §4.1.2 must be re-scoped — the largest item, larger than the voting change itself

`auto-option.md:209-212` (codex `:136-143`, etc `:143-150`) escalates one rung when the spread between
the highest and lowest member confidence is ≥ 0.3 — **even when the committee is unanimous** — and
halts as critical when the committee is already at terminal size.

This rule is built entirely on the ladder being removed. Two silent failure modes:

- Left as written **with no rung at all**, every committee is terminal, so any spread ≥ 0.3 becomes an
  immediate critical HALT. Members self-reporting 0.9 and 0.6 is routine. This is strictly *more*
  halting than today, in the mode the user asked to make lighter — a far larger behavioural change
  than simple majority, in the opposite direction from the intent.
- Left as written **alongside Q3's tie rung**, the rung becomes a general escalation-on-disagreement
  path again, because confidence spread correlates with vote splits — restoring through the back door
  precisely the ladder the user deleted.

**Decision: fold it into §4.1.1's threshold mechanism.** A wide spread adjusts the adopted decision's
confidence downward; only a sub-threshold result escalates to critical. No re-vote on spread alone.
~~The rung fires on absence of a majority, and on nothing else.~~ **Superseded by `08` §2 (5-0):** there is no rung; absence of a majority escalates to `critical` and halts for the user. Spread alone still never escalates.

This supersedes an earlier provisional decision, made before the committee reported, that §4.1.2
should escalate to a critical HALT. That was wrong for the reason above.

### 4.2 §3:159 forbids the change as written

> 금지: 위원회가 만장일치도 최다 득표도 아닌 임의 결정을 채택(사다리 위반) — 즉시 HALT.

A recommended fast-path decision is neither unanimous nor a plurality — it has no votes at all.
~~A lead-member tie-break is not a plurality either.~~ **Superseded by `08` §2 (5-0):** no lead-member tie-break ships, so the reworded line must not admit one. The recommended fast path alone still makes this line forbid the feature being
added. It must be reworded in the same edit or the SSOT self-refutes and a compliant agent must HALT
on every fast-pathed gate.

### 4.3 Two bypass mechanisms need a stated precedence

`default_if_auto` is already a committee bypass (`:126`, `:283` — "서브에이전트 우회 가능 — fast path"),
scoped to `severity=clarification` at confidence 1.0, and narrowed further in the `etc` rendering
(`:202`) to low-risk clarification only. The new `recommended` marker is severity-agnostic. Two
bypasses with different scopes and no ranking is the likeliest source of the next contradiction.

**Decision: `recommended` wins, then `default_if_auto`, then the committee.** This follows the suite's
own precedent for the shape — §11.1, where the specific `--auto-apply` beats the broad `--auto`, and
`loop-option.md:24`, where an explicit `--loops` beats the preset `--mini`.

### 4.4 The marker must be a structured field, never a prose scan

No recommendation marker exists anywhere today. The only structured option schema in the suite is
`kiwi-pm`'s NEEDS_USER payload (`SKILL.md:468-482`), whose options carry `{key,label,consequence}`.

Three informal `(권장)` labels exist, all in `kiwi-pm` (`:553`, `:643`, `:836`), and nothing parses
them. **Two of them read "(A) HALT — kiwi-srs-feasibility 실행 후 재시도 (권장)".** A fast path that
scanned label text would auto-adopt a recommended **HALT**. Harmless in that instance, and proof that
the annotation was never written for machine consumption.

**Decision: add `recommended: true` to the one structured schema that exists, opt-in and unmarked by
default; state explicitly that an unparsed prose `(권장)` confers nothing.** Then no gate declaration
site is forced to change, and the fast path starts empty rather than firing on legacy prose.

A consequence worth stating plainly: because the marker starts unused, the recommended fast path contributes nothing at first; ~~**the only behavioural change users will actually feel at first is the 3-member simple majority.**~~
**Superseded by `08` §2 (5-0):** there are **two** behavioural changes users feel at first — the 3-member simple majority, and the **halt for the user** on a gate the committee cannot resolve (`08:48-49`),
which under Q3's rung would have resolved without stopping the run. That is what makes Q1 and Q3 the consequential decisions and Q2 the one where the evidence favoured doing nothing.

### 4.5 The audit trail goes blind exactly where scrutiny was least

`merge_method.rule` (`:157`) and the §10 log schema (`:329-359`) enumerate
`unanimous` / `plurality` / `tie-break-lead`, and `committee_votes[]` is required. A fast-pathed
decision has zero votes, no confidence, and no committee size. Without an explicit shape it is not
recorded at all — so the decisions that received **no deliberation** are precisely the ones that leave
**no evidence**.

**Decision: extend the schema with
`{"rule": "recommended-fastpath", "committee_size": 0, "marked_by": "<gate declaration site>"}`.**
Note also that `unanimous` stops being a *rule* and becomes an *outcome* once majority decides.

### 4.6 §3.1's quorum rule degrades badly at three members

`:165` lets a failed member be dropped and the committee proceed if a majority quorum remains. With
three members and one dropped, two remain, and a 1-1 among them has no majority — so it falls
straight to the lead member, quietly becoming single-agent decision-making.

**Decision: the degraded-quorum case escalates under the same rule as a tie.**

### 4.7 The `etc` profile carve-out must survive verbatim

`local-llm-profile.md:20-22` makes the `etc` rendering behave "as if `--max` is enabled" by default,
but `:26` and `:54` explicitly carve the committee out of that default. If the carve-out is lost in
the rewrite, every `etc` host silently jumps to a 5-member sequential committee on every gate.

## 7. Two proposals deferred, recorded so they are not lost

A member proposed a compensator for simple majority that costs nothing and was on no ballot: on a
non-unanimous vote, require the main session to read the **dissenting** member's `side_effects[]` and
route any side effect the majority did not name into §4.1.1's confidence adjustment.

The evidence for it is a real case in this repository. The one recorded 3-member `--auto` split —
`.kiwi/sessions/2026-06-17.speckiwi.v3-0-0/reports/decision-T-PH003-06.md` — was unanimous on the
facts and 2-1 on the mechanism, and was resolved correctly only because the dissenter's risk was
independently confirmed *after* the vote, at which point a third and safer mechanism was adopted. The
remedy that worked was post-vote review of the dissent, not more voters. The 5-member record at
`docs/analysis/kiwi-committee-2026-07-12.v2301-decisions/synthesis.json` is consistent: 3 of 5
decisions unanimous, 2 split, and both splits landed on the highest-consequence items.

A second deferred proposal: a per-gate confidence threshold override (externally visible gates require
≥ 0.85, sub-threshold escalates to critical), which would buy a safety margin proportional to
consequence without converting any gate into an unconditional halt. This is the better-targeted dial
than `critical_gates[]` for the Q2 population, and is the change to make if the relaxed voting rule
later needs insuring.

Neither is in `2.6.0-phase1-kiwi-orchestrator` scope. Both are recorded here so a later session does not have to rediscover
them.
