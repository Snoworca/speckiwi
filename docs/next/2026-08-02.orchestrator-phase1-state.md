## 5e. The mutation ledger, and the two readings of the criterion it discharges

`test/skills/orchestrator-mutation-ledger.json` — 31 `rule_id`s × 3 variants = 93 rows, 66 carrier
anchors covering all 22 modal carriers in each variant. Every one of the 93 mutations was applied,
turned the harness red, and had its bytes restored. Seven anti-vacuity probes against the harness —
empty ledger, row removed, snippet unresolvable, wrong `heading_path`, cross-variant parity broken,
undeclared mutation class, named-sentence row with no owner — are all red.

**Two things had to be decided while building it, and both are judgment calls worth flagging.**

First, `05` §10.3 does not require a mutation to overlap a carrier snippet; that constraint was
invented while authoring the harness and it is wrong. The normative force of a Korean sentence is
often carried by the `-지 않는다` suffix while the `금지` token sits in a neighbouring clause of the
same sentence, and §10.3's carrier set has no entry for the suffix. The harness now ties a mutation
to its rule by **line**, not by byte overlap.

Second, most of the rules these eight requirements own carry **no** member of §10.3's modal set at
all — they are ordering pairs and quantifiers. Under §10.3's literal definition ("a normative
sentence is one carrying a member of the modal set") five of the eight add *no* normative sentence
and their criterion is **vacuous**. The criterion's own structure argues against that reading: its
two clauses are joined by "and", and if "normative sentence" were defined by modal carriers the
second clause — *"every modal carrier in those sentences is covered by a ledger row's carrier
anchor"* — would be redundant. The requirements' own evidence notes also already treat ordering and
quantifier sentences as mutation-bearing. **The broad reading is the one taken here, and it is
recorded rather than assumed.** An audit of per-requirement completeness is outstanding before any of
the eight criteria is ticked.

**Six rules are `unowned`** — normative sentences the shipped skill states with no requirement behind
them: the `/snoworca-*` call ban, the commit-signature ban, the changelog-in-body ban, and the run
contract's no-editing-a-completed-unit, no-weakening-tests and no-writing-outside-the-lease. In a
repository whose first rule is that no behaviour ships without a requirement, six of them in one
shipped skill body is a finding, not a labelling detail.
